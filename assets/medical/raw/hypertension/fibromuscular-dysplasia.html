Key Features: FMD vs. Atherosclerotic Renal Artery Stenosis

Feature Fibromuscular Dysplasia (FMD) Atherosclerotic RAS (ARAS)
Typical Patient Young–middle-aged women (20–50 y) Older adults (>50 y), often with diffuse atherosclerosis
Angiographic Pattern "String of beads" — mid-to-distal renal artery Focal, ostial/proximal stenosis
Duplex PSV Often markedly elevated (e.g., 230 cm/s in this case) Elevated (typically 200–300 cm/s)
Treatment of Choice PTRA (balloon angioplasty alone) Medical therapy; PTRA/stent only in select cases
Outcome after PTRA Hypertension cured in ~37%, improved in ~80% Less responsive to revascularization

Check Your Understanding

1. A 36-year-old woman has persistent postpartum hypertension. Renal duplex shows a PSV of 230 cm/s in the right renal artery. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. FMD is the most common cause of renovascular hypertension in young women. The markedly elevated PSV (230 cm/s) and the mid-portion location are classic for FMD. Atherosclerotic RAS typically affects older patients and the ostial/proximal artery.

2. What is the treatment of choice for renovascular hypertension due to FMD?

Correct: B. PTRA (balloon angioplasty alone, without stenting) is the treatment of choice for FMD. It is curative in a significant proportion of patients and avoids the need for lifelong antihypertensive medication. Stenting is reserved for procedural complications.

3. Which angiographic finding is characteristic of FMD?

Correct: B. The "string of beads" appearance reflects alternating areas of stenosis (from fibroplasia) and post-stenotic dilatation. It is the classic angiographic hallmark of medial fibroplasia, the most common type of FMD.

4. Why should ACE inhibitors be used with caution in renal artery stenosis?

Correct: B. Angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole to maintain GFR when renal perfusion is low. ACE inhibitors block this compensation, causing efferent dilation and a precipitous drop in GFR — especially in bilateral RAS or a solitary kidney.

5. What PSV threshold on renal duplex ultrasound suggests a hemodynamically significant stenosis (>60%)?

Correct: B. A PSV of ≥200 cm/s (or a renal-aortic ratio ≥3.5) is the standard duplex criterion for a hemodynamically significant stenosis (>60%). This patient's PSV of 230 cm/s clearly exceeds this threshold.
