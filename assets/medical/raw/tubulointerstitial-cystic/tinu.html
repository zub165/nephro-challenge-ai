Key Features: Differential Diagnosis of Interstitial Nephritis

Disease Typical Patient Histology Extra-renal Features Key Clue
TINU Syndrome Correct Adolescents & young adults (median age ~15–25) Acute interstitial nephritis with mixed infiltrate (lymphocytes, plasma cells, eosinophils) + tubulitis Bilateral anterior uveitis (painful red eyes, photophobia, blurred vision) Young patient + AIN + uveitis = TINU; often responds to steroids
Drug-Induced AIN Distractor Any age; recent exposure to NSAIDs, antibiotics, PPIs Interstitial infiltrate with prominent eosinophils; may have granulomas Fever, rash, eosinophilia (may form a triad) Recent drug exposure; eosinophils on biopsy; resolves after drug withdrawal
Sjögren Syndrome Distractor Older women; known or suspected autoimmune disease Chronic interstitial nephritis with tubular atrophy and fibrosis; often CD4+ T-cell infiltrate Dry eyes, dry mouth (sicca), arthritis, Raynaud's, anti-SSA/SSB antibodies Sicca symptoms + chronic AIN + positive autoantibodies
Sarcoidosis Distractor Adults 20–40; often Black or Northern European Non-caseating granulomas in interstitium; may have nephrocalcinosis Hilar adenopathy, uveitis, erythema nodosum, hypercalcemia Bilateral hilar adenopathy + uveitis + AIN + hypercalcemia
Renal Tuberculosis Distractor Patients from endemic regions or immunocompromised Caseating granulomas with Langhans giant cells; acid-fast bacilli Pulmonary TB, constitutional symptoms, sterile pyuria Sterile pyuria + hematuria + caseating granulomas + positive AFB culture
Viral AIN Distractor Any age; often immunocompromised (CMV) or children (parvovirus) Mild interstitial infiltrate; viral inclusions (smudge cells for CMV) Depends on virus — CMV (colitis, pneumonitis), parvovirus (arthralgia, rash) Viral inclusion bodies; positive PCR/serology; often self-limited

Check Your Understanding

1. A 28-year-old man presents with malaise, bilateral painful red eyes, and AKI (creatinine 3.8 mg/dL). Urinalysis shows sterile pyuria and granular casts. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. The combination of acute interstitial nephritis (sterile pyuria, granular casts, AKI) with bilateral anterior uveitis (painful red eyes) in a young adult is diagnostic of TINU syndrome.

2. Which histologic finding is most characteristic of TINU on kidney biopsy?

Correct: A. TINU is a form of acute interstitial nephritis. Biopsy typically shows a dense interstitial infiltrate of lymphocytes, plasma cells, and often eosinophils, with tubulitis. Caseating granulomas suggest TB, and subepithelial deposits suggest glomerulonephritis.

3. What is the initial management step in TINU syndrome?

Correct: B. The first step is to discontinue any potentially offending drugs (NSAIDs, antibiotics) that could be contributing to the AIN. If kidney function does not improve or the uveitis is significant, systemic corticosteroids are then indicated.

4. Which of the following is the most likely cause of drug-induced AIN?

Correct: B. The most common causes of drug-induced AIN are NSAIDs, proton-pump inhibitors, and certain antibiotics (penicillins, cephalosporins, rifampin, sulfonamides). Other drugs can also cause it, but these are the classic offenders.

5. A patient has chronic interstitial nephritis with fibrosis and sicca symptoms. Which antibody is most likely to be positive?

Correct: C. Chronic interstitial nephritis with sicca symptoms (dry eyes, dry mouth) is characteristic of Sjögren syndrome, which is associated with anti-SSA (Ro) and anti-SSB (La) antibodies.

6. Which finding on kidney biopsy is most consistent with renal tuberculosis?

Correct: C. Renal TB is characterized by caseating granulomas with Langhans giant cells, along with sterile pyuria and hematuria. Non-caseating granulomas are more typical of sarcoidosis.
