Anion Gap Visualizer

The anion gap reflects unmeasured anions in the blood. Normal = 8–12. Elevation indicates accumulation of unmeasured acids (lactate, ketones, salicylate, oxalate, formate, etc.).

Check Your Understanding

1. A 55-year-old man presents after aspirin overdose with pH 7.48, PCO₂ 21, HCO₃⁻ 18, and anion gap 18. Which metabolic abnormality is expected?

Correct: B. At high doses, salicylates are uricosuric — they inhibit URAT1 in the proximal tubule, causing increased uric acid excretion and hypouricemia. This is a well-recognized but often overlooked effect of salicylate poisoning.

2. What type of acid–base disorder is most characteristic of acute salicylate toxicity?

Correct: C. Salicylates cause a classic mixed disorder: they directly stimulate the respiratory center (respiratory alkalosis) and also cause accumulation of salicylate, ketones, and lactate (high-anion gap metabolic acidosis). The pH may be normal, elevated, or reduced depending on the dominant process.

3. Why does this patient have a low serum potassium despite a shift of K⁺ into cells from alkalosis?

Correct: B. The hypokalemia in salicylate toxicity is multifactorial: respiratory alkalosis shifts K⁺ into cells, and renal losses occur from secondary hyperaldosteronism (from volume depletion) and possibly vomiting. It is not just a shift.

4. Which laboratory pattern is expected in salicylate toxicity?

Correct: B. The classic triad of salicylate toxicity is: elevated anion gap (from salicylate, ketones, lactate), hypokalemia (from shifts + renal losses), and hypouricemia (from uricosuric effect of salicylates).

5. Which of the following statements about the respiratory alkalosis in salicylate toxicity is TRUE?

Correct: A. Unlike the compensatory hyperventilation of metabolic acidosis, the respiratory alkalosis in salicylate toxicity is due to direct stimulation of the medullary respiratory center by salicylate. This is why the PCO₂ is often lower than expected for the degree of metabolic acidosis.

6. A patient with a high anion gap metabolic acidosis has calcium oxalate crystals in the urine and an elevated osmolar gap. What is the most likely toxin?

Correct: C. Ethylene glycol toxicity is classically associated with calcium oxalate crystals in the urine, an elevated osmolar gap, and a high anion gap metabolic acidosis. Methanol also causes an elevated osmolar gap but does not produce oxalate crystals — it causes visual disturbances.
