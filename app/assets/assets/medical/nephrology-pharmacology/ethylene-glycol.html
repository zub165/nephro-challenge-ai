Physiology: Ethanol and ethylene glycol compete for alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH). Ethanol has a higher affinity for ADH, so it is preferentially metabolized. This blocks conversion of ethylene glycol into toxic acids (glycolic acid, oxalic acid), preventing the high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis and oxalate nephropathy. The unmetabolized ethylene glycol stays in circulation — producing the osmolar gap — and is slowly excreted unchanged by the kidneys.

Check Your Understanding

1. A patient has an ethylene glycol level of 200 mg/dL but a normal anion gap (10) and normal bicarbonate (24). The measured osmolar gap is 70 mOsm/L. Why is there no metabolic acidosis?

Correct: E. Ethanol competitively inhibits ADH, preventing ethylene glycol from being metabolized into glycolic and oxalic acid. Without these toxic metabolites, no high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis develops. Additionally, the osmolar gap math supports ethanol: EG accounts for only 32 of the 70 mOsm/L gap, leaving 38 mOsm/L — which corresponds to ~175 mg/dL ethanol.

2. A patient with ethylene glycol toxicity has an osmolar gap of 70 mOsm/L. Ethylene glycol accounts for 32 mOsm/L (200 mg/dL ÷ 6.2). What accounts for the remaining 38 mOsm/L?

Correct: C. The 38 mOsm/L gap corresponds to ~175 mg/dL ethanol (38 × 4.6 = 175). Ethanol contributes to the osmolar gap but does not generate an anion gap (it is metabolized to acetic acid, which is quickly converted to CO₂ and water). This also explains why the anion gap is normal — ethanol is blocking ethylene glycol metabolism.

3. What is the mechanism by which ethanol prevents ethylene glycol-induced metabolic acidosis?

Correct: B. Ethanol and ethylene glycol are both substrates for alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH). Ethanol has a higher affinity for ADH, so it is preferentially metabolized. This blocks the conversion of ethylene glycol into its toxic metabolites (glycolic and oxalic acid) — the same mechanism used therapeutically with fomepizole or ethanol infusion.

4. If this patient had NOT co-ingested ethanol, what laboratory pattern would you expect 12–24 hours after ingestion?

Correct: C. Without ethanol, ethylene glycol is rapidly metabolized to glycolic acid (and oxalic acid), producing a high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis. The osmolar gap decreases as the parent alcohol is consumed. This is the classic stage 2 presentation of ethylene glycol toxicity.

5. Which treatment works by the same mechanism as the ethanol in this patient's co-ingestion?

Correct: C. Fomepizole is a potent inhibitor of alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) — it works by the same mechanism as ethanol but is safer, more predictable, and does not cause intoxication. Both are used as antidotes for ethylene glycol and methanol poisoning. Hemodialysis removes the toxic alcohol and metabolites, but is a different mechanism.

6. Why does the anion gap remain normal when only the parent alcohol is circulating?

Correct: A. Ethylene glycol is a neutral alcohol — it is osmotically active but not an acid. It does not contribute unmeasured anions, so the anion gap is normal. Only when it is metabolized to glycolic acid and oxalic acid does the anion gap rise. This is why the early stage of ethylene glycol toxicity shows a high osmolar gap but normal anion gap.
