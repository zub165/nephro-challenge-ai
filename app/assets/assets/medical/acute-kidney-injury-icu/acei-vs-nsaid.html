Understanding the Diagram

Click the buttons above to see the changes.

  • Normal: Prostaglandins keep the afferent open. Angiotensin II keeps the efferent slightly tight, maintaining a healthy GFR.
  • Bilateral RAS: The afferent is blocked by stenosis. The kidney compensates by massively increasing Angiotensin II to clamp the efferent shut, keeping pressure high enough to survive.
  • Add ACEi: The efferent loses its Angiotensin II tone and dilates. Pressure crashes, and GFR falls (Creatinine rises).
  • Add NSAID: Prostaglandins are blocked, causing the afferent to clamp shut. Less blood enters, pressure drops, and GFR falls.

The "Double Hit"

Why ACEi + NSAID is dangerous

  • NSAID: Squeezes the entrance (Afferent).
  • ACEi: Opens the exit (Efferent).
  • Combining them causes a catastrophic drop in intraglomerular pressure, leading to acute kidney injury.
