Key Features: Acute Flank Pain & Fever in ADPKD

Complication Urinalysis Imaging Treatment Key Clue
Cyst Infection Correct WBCs, bacteria (if communicating) CT/MRI; PET if inconclusive Lipophilic antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, TMP-SMX, clindamycin) for 4–6 weeks Fever, flank pain in ADPKD; urine culture may be negative if cyst does not communicate
Pyelonephritis Distractor WBCs, WBC casts, bacteria CT if severe or no improvement Fluoroquinolones or IV cephalosporins; 7–14 days Ascending infection; responds to standard UTI antibiotics

Check Your Understanding

1. A 45-year-old woman with ADPKD presents with fever, right flank pain, and urinalysis showing WBCs and gram-negative rods. What is the best next step?

Correct: C. The patient has a urinary tract infection likely involving the kidney (pyelonephritis or cyst infection). Ciprofloxacin is the best choice because it is a fluoroquinolone that penetrates renal cysts well, unlike beta-lactams (ceftriaxone). It also covers gram-negative rods. She is stable, so oral therapy is appropriate.

2. Why is ciprofloxacin preferred over ceftriaxone in ADPKD with suspected cyst infection?

Correct: B. In ADPKD, cyst infections are difficult to treat because many antibiotics do not penetrate the cyst wall. Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin) are lipophilic and achieve good concentrations in cyst fluid. Beta-lactams such as ceftriaxone do not penetrate cysts well.

3. What is the recommended duration of antibiotic therapy for renal cyst infection in ADPKD?

Correct: C. Cyst infections in ADPKD require prolonged therapy (4–6 weeks) because the cyst environment is poorly penetrated and infections are difficult to eradicate. Shorter courses risk relapse.

4. When is imaging (CT or MRI) indicated in ADPKD with suspected cyst infection?

Correct: B. Imaging is not required before starting antibiotics in a stable patient with a clear urinary source. However, if the diagnosis is uncertain, the patient fails to improve after 48–72 hours, or complications (abscess, obstruction) are suspected, CT or MRI should be obtained. PET/CT is useful when CT is inconclusive.

5. Which of the following is a common cause of acute flank pain in ADPKD that is NOT infectious?

Correct: B. Cyst hemorrhage is a common non-infectious cause of acute flank pain in ADPKD. It presents with sudden severe pain, often with gross hematuria, and is usually managed conservatively. Cyst infection and pyelonephritis are infectious causes.

6. In a patient with ADPKD and suspected cyst infection, which imaging modality is most sensitive for detecting infected cysts?

Correct: C. PET/CT with FDG is the most sensitive imaging modality for detecting infected cysts in ADPKD. It can identify increased metabolic activity in the cyst wall. CT and MRI may show cyst wall thickening or debris but are less specific. PET is particularly useful when CT is inconclusive.
