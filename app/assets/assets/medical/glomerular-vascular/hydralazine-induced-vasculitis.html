Key Features: Differential Diagnosis of ANCA Vasculitis

Condition Serology Biopsy Pattern Target Organs Key Distinguisher
Hydralazine-Induced Vasculitis Correct MPO-ANCA + antihistone Pauci-immune crescentic GN Kidney, lung, joints, skin Long-term hydralazine exposure (≥1 year); co-positive antihistone antibodies; improves with drug withdrawal
Primary ANCA Vasculitis (MPA / GPA) Distractor MPO-ANCA OR PR3-ANCA (single positive) Pauci-immune crescentic GN ± granulomas Kidney, lung, sinus, nerves, skin No drug exposure; no antihistone antibodies
Drug-Induced Lupus (Hydralazine) Distractor Antihistone (+ ANA) Typically NO renal involvement Joints, skin, serosa Anti-dsDNA usually negative; low complement uncommon; renal involvement rare
Dual-Positive (MPO-ANCA + anti-GBM) Distractor MPO-ANCA + anti-GBM Crescentic GN with LINEAR IgG on IF Kidney + lung (pulmonary-renal) No drug history required; anti-GBM on IF is the distinguishing feature

Check Your Understanding

1. A 68-year-old man on hydralazine for 3 years presents with AKI, RBC casts, pulmonary infiltrates, and arthralgias. Which serology is most consistent with hydralazine-associated vasculitis?

Correct: D. Hydralazine is the classic drug associated with MPO-ANCA–positive vasculitis AND antihistone antibodies. The combination is virtually diagnostic.

2. What is the most common ANCA type in hydralazine-induced vasculitis?

Correct: B. Hydralazine preferentially causes MPO-ANCA. PR3-ANCA is more typical of primary GPA.

3. Why is antihistone antibody important in this case?

Correct: B. Antihistone antibodies are present in >95% of drug-induced lupus cases.

4. What is the most important first step in managing hydralazine-induced vasculitis?

Correct: B. The most important first step is to stop hydralazine. Many patients improve with drug withdrawal alone.

5. Which finding would argue AGAINST hydralazine-induced vasculitis?

Correct: D. Drug-induced lupus is associated with antihistone (not anti-dsDNA) and typically has normal complements. Anti-dsDNA + low complement suggests idiopathic SLE.

6. Which best differentiates hydralazine-induced vasculitis from primary MPO-ANCA vasculitis?

Correct: C. Both share kidney involvement, MPO-ANCA positivity, and pulmonary hemorrhage. The key differentiators are co-positivity for antihistone antibodies and a history of long-term hydralazine exposure.
