Key Features: Differential Diagnosis

Disease Light Microscopy (LM) Immunofluorescence (IF) Electron Microscopy (EM) Clinical / Lab Clues
HIVICK Correct Proliferative GN (often MPGN pattern), endocapillary proliferation Granular IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, C1q ("full house") Subendothelial and subepithelial electron-dense deposits; tubuloreticular aggregates Low C3/C4, active sediment (dysmorphic RBCs), proteinuria, HIV+
HIVAN Distractor Collapsing glomerulopathy, podocyte hypertrophy, tubular microcysts Negative or trace (no immune complexes) Foot process effacement, tubuloreticular inclusions (TRIs), collapsed GBM Normal complement, heavy proteinuria, bland sediment, advanced HIV

Check Your Understanding

1. Which serological finding best distinguishes HIVICK from HIVAN?

Correct: B. HIVICK is an immune complex disease, so it consumes complement and causes low C3/C4. HIVAN is a direct viral cytopathic effect and typically has normal complement levels.

2. Which finding on electron microscopy is characteristic of HIVAN?

Correct: B. Tubuloreticular inclusions (TRIs) are the hallmark EM finding in HIVAN. They are also known as "interferon footprints" and help distinguish HIVAN from idiopathic collapsing glomerulopathy.

3. Which disease is characterized by crescents in Bowman's space?

Correct: B. Crescentic GN is defined by the formation of crescents (proliferating parietal epithelial cells and macrophages) in Bowman's space, which compress the glomerular tuft.

4. Which disease shows normal light microscopy and negative immunofluorescence, but abnormal electron microscopy?

Correct: C. MCD shows normal light microscopy and negative IF. The only abnormality is diffuse foot process effacement on EM, causing nephrotic-range proteinuria without hematuria.
