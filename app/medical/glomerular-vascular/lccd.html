🟣 LCDD — Pathogenesis

Monoclonal light chain deposition → nodular glomerulosclerosis

  • Underlying cause: Plasma cell dyscrasia (≈50% have multiple myeloma) producing an abnormal monoclonal light chain
  • Deposition: Light chains deposit along glomerular basement membrane, tubular basement membrane, and mesangium
  • IF: Monotypic κ or λ light chain — linear or granular staining along basement membranes
  • EM: Nonorganized, granular, electron-dense material — "ground pepper-like" deposits
  • Congo red: NEGATIVE (distinguishes from amyloidosis)

🟣 LCDD — Clinical Features

Nephrotic-range proteinuria + progressive renal failure

  • Demographics: Adults, average age 55–60 years
  • Presentation: Proteinuria (often nephrotic range), hematuria, hypertension, reduced GFR
  • LM: Nodular mesangial sclerosis, tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis
  • EM hallmark: Granular electron-dense deposits along basement membranes — identified in all renal biopsies
  • Prognosis: Progressive CKD; median renal survival ~5.4 years from diagnosis
🟣

Board Pearl — Granular EM Deposits + Monotypic Light Chain = LCDD

The nonorganized granular electron-dense deposits on EM are the key discriminator from amyloidosis (which shows fibrillar deposits). Combined with monotypic κ light chain on IF and Congo red negative staining, this is classic light chain deposition disease. About half of patients have underlying multiple myeloma.
