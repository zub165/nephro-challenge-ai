Interpretation: The calculated urine osmolality (140 + 160 = 300 mOsm/L) exactly matches the measured value. This rules out mannitol (which would raise measured above calculated) and rules out diabetes insipidus (which would produce a much more dilute urine). The urine is iso-osmolar because it is dominated by the obligatory solute load from urea, driven by high-dose steroids → protein catabolism → increased urea production. This is solute diuresis from urea.

Check Your Understanding

1. A patient with polyuria (4 L/day) and hypernatremia has a urine osmolality of 300 mOsm/L. Which diagnosis is MOST likely?

Correct: C. An iso-osmolar urine (~300 mOsm/L) argues against both forms of diabetes insipidus (which produce dilute urine). Combined with a very high urine urea nitrogen (448 mg/dL) in a post-op patient on high-dose steroids, this is classic for solute diuresis from urea.

2. Calculate the urine osmolality: Urine Na⁺ 48, Urine K⁺ 22, Urine urea nitrogen 448 mg/dL. What is the total?

Correct: C. Electrolytes: 2 × (48 + 22) = 140 mOsm/L. Urea: 448 ÷ 2.8 = 160 mOsm/L. Total = 140 + 160 = 300 mOsm/L. This matches the measured value exactly.

3. What does the "2" represent in the formula 2 × (Urine Na⁺ + Urine K⁺)?

Correct: A. Urine osmolality from electrolytes is estimated by multiplying the sum of measured cations (Na⁺ + K⁺) by 2, because equal numbers of unmeasured anions (chloride, bicarbonate, phosphate, sulfate) must be present to maintain electroneutrality. Urea and glucose are uncharged and are added separately.

4. What does the "2.8" represent when converting urine urea nitrogen (mg/dL) to mOsm/L?

Correct: B. Urea contains 2 nitrogen atoms (2 × 14 = 28 g/mol N). Converting mg/dL to mmol/L requires dividing by (28 ÷ 10) = 2.8. This gives the molar concentration of urea, which equals its osmolar contribution (since urea is a neutral molecule).

5. If the measured urine osmolality were 450 mOsm/L but the calculated osmolality was only 300 mOsm/L, what would this suggest?

Correct: D. When the measured urine osmolality is higher than the calculated (electrolytes + urea + glucose), the difference is due to an unmeasured osmole — mannitol is the classic example. In this case, the measured and calculated values match, ruling out mannitol.

6. Why do high-dose steroids cause solute diuresis from urea?

Correct: B. Glucocorticoids promote protein catabolism, which increases urea production from amino acid breakdown. The elevated urea load is filtered and excreted, obligating water loss (osmotic diuresis) → polyuria → hypernatremia. This is why the urine urea nitrogen is markedly elevated at 448 mg/dL.
