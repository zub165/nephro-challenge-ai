Urine Anion Gap (UAG) — Animated Visualizer

The urine anion gap estimates ammonium (NH₄⁺) excretion. In metabolic acidosis, the kidney should increase NH₄⁺ excretion. A negative UAG means the kidney is responding appropriately (extrarenal HCO₃⁻ loss, e.g., diarrhea). A positive UAG means the kidney is NOT excreting enough NH₄⁺ (RTA).

UAG = (Urine Na⁺ + Urine K⁺) − Urine Cl⁻

Check Your Understanding

1. A patient with Sjögren's syndrome presents with metabolic acidosis (HCO₃⁻ 14 mEq/L), hypokalemia (K⁺ 2.9 mEq/L), and a urine pH of 6.5. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. Type 1 (distal) RTA is characterized by the inability to acidify the urine (urine pH > 5.5) despite systemic acidosis, hypokalemia, and a normal anion gap metabolic acidosis. Sjögren's syndrome is a classic autoimmune cause.

2. A patient with HIV on tenofovir presents with proximal muscle weakness, glycosuria (normal serum glucose), and hypophosphatemia. Labs show HCO₃⁻ 18 mEq/L and K⁺ 3.0 mEq/L. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. Tenofovir is a well-known cause of proximal tubular toxicity (Fanconi syndrome), presenting with Type 2 RTA, glycosuria, phosphaturia, and hypokalemia. The normal serum glucose and hypophosphatemia distinguish this from diabetic ketoacidosis.

3. A 62-year-old man with type 2 diabetes and CKD presents with persistent hyperkalemia (K⁺ 5.8 mEq/L) and mild metabolic acidosis (HCO₃⁻ 19 mEq/L). He is on lisinopril and spironolactone. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: C. Type 4 RTA is the most common RTA in adults and is characterized by hyperkalemia and a mild normal anion gap metabolic acidosis. It is caused by aldosterone deficiency or resistance. Diabetes, ACE inhibitors (lisinopril), and potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone) are classic contributors.

4. In a patient with non-anion gap metabolic acidosis, the urine anion gap is calculated as (urine Na⁺ + urine K⁺) − urine Cl⁻. Which UAG result suggests RTA rather than diarrhea?

Correct: B. A positive UAG (more unmeasured anions than cations) indicates that the kidney is not excreting enough ammonium (NH₄⁺) to handle the acid load, which is characteristic of RTA. A negative UAG indicates appropriate renal ammonium excretion, which is seen in extrarenal bicarbonate losses like diarrhea. The UAG estimates ammonium excretion indirectly in the setting of chronic metabolic acidosis.

5. What is the first-line treatment for Type 1 (distal) RTA with hypokalemia?

Correct: B. Type 1 RTA is treated with potassium citrate (1–3 mEq/kg/day) to correct both the acidosis and the hypokalemia. Sodium bicarbonate is an alternative if potassium is normal or high. Fludrocortisone is used in Type 4 RTA, and furosemide is used in some cases of hyperkalemia but is not first-line for Type 1.
