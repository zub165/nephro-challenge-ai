✅ Do This

The correct combination in acute TLS

  • Rasburicase — rapidly converts uric acid to allantoin (soluble)
  • IV normal saline — 2–3× maintenance to maintain high urine output
  • Monitor K⁺, PO₄, Ca every 4–6 hours
  • Consider dialysis for refractory hyperkalemia, severe hyperphosphatemia, or oliguric AKI
  • Target UOP ≥ 80–100 mL/m²/h

🚫 Do NOT Do

Common mistakes in TLS management

  • Routine bicarbonate — worsens calcium-phosphate precipitation → metastatic calcification
  • Combine allopurinol + rasburicase — redundant; increases xanthine nephropathy risk
  • High-dose febuxostat — no role in acute TLS; slower than rasburicase
  • Calcium supplementation — only if symptomatic; Ca binds PO₄ → tissue deposits
  • Delaying dialysis — if oliguric AKI + severe electrolyte derangement
💧

Board Pearl — Saline is the Answer

In TLS, the answer to "what do you give with rasburicase?" is almost always IV normal saline. Bicarbonate has fallen out of favor because it worsens calcium-phosphate precipitation in tissues. Rasburicase handles the uric acid; saline flushes the system.

✅ Quick Check — 5 Questions

1. In addition to rasburicase, what is the most appropriate next step in TLS?

IV normal saline. Aggressive hydration maintains urine output and prevents crystal deposition — the cornerstone of TLS management.

2. Why should bicarbonate be avoided in this patient?

Correct. Alkalinizing the urine in the presence of hyperphosphatemia + hypocalcemia promotes calcium-phosphate deposition in tissues → metastatic calcification.

3. What is the target urine output in acute TLS?

80–100 mL/m²/h. High urine output prevents uric acid and calcium-phosphate crystal deposition in renal tubules.

4. Why should allopurinol NOT be added to rasburicase?

Correct. Both lower uric acid, but allopurinol blocks xanthine oxidase → xanthine accumulates (poorly soluble → nephropathy). Rasburicase alone is sufficient.

5. Which lab pattern best characterizes TLS?

↑K⁺, ↑PO₄, ↓Ca, ↑uric acid. Classic TLS tetrad — hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia (from Ca-PO₄ precipitation), and hyperuricemia.
