Check Your Understanding

1. A 20-year-old woman with neurogenic bladder since childhood presents with proteinuria (3.5 g/d), CKD (Cr 2.0), and biopsy showing interstitial fibrosis + FSGS NOS. What is the most likely cause?

Correct: A. Chronic obstruction from her undiagnosed neurogenic bladder caused obstructive nephropathy with nephron loss and interstitial fibrosis. The remaining nephrons underwent adaptive hyperfiltration → secondary FSGS (NOS). The sub-nephrotic proteinuria, CKD, HTN, and interstitial fibrosis on biopsy all support this.

2. How does chronic obstructive nephropathy lead to FSGS?

Correct: B. Obstruction causes tubulointerstitial injury with nephron loss. The remaining nephrons compensate with hyperfiltration (increased single-nephron GFR), which over time causes glomerular sclerosis — this is the pathway to secondary FSGS.

3. Why is this patient's FSGS classified as SECONDARY rather than primary?

Correct: B. Primary FSGS is idiopathic (thought to be driven by a circulating permeability factor) and lacks an identifiable cause. Secondary FSGS has a known underlying driver — here, chronic obstruction leading to nephron loss. The presence of interstitial fibrosis on biopsy supports the secondary (adaptive) nature, as opposed to primary FSGS which typically shows diffuse foot process effacement without fibrosis.

4. What is the appropriate treatment for this patient's secondary FSGS?

Correct: C. Secondary FSGS does NOT respond to immunosuppression. The mainstay of treatment is RAAS blockade (ACEi or ARB) to reduce intraglomerular pressure and slow CKD progression, plus blood pressure control. Steroids and other immunosuppressants are used for primary FSGS.

5. Which finding would most strongly argue AGAINST a diagnosis of obesity-related FSGS in this patient?

Correct: C. Obesity-related FSGS typically occurs in patients with BMI > 30 kg/m². This patient's BMI is 21, which is normal — making obesity-related hyperfiltration injury very unlikely. Additionally, the presence of interstitial fibrosis and neurogenic bladder points away from obesity as the cause.

6. Why does the interstitial fibrosis on this patient's biopsy suggest a chronic, secondary process rather than primary FSGS?

Correct: A. Primary FSGS typically presents with diffuse foot process effacement without significant tubulointerstitial fibrosis. The presence of moderate interstitial fibrosis suggests a chronic process with nephron loss — here, obstructive nephropathy — leading to secondary (adaptive) FSGS.
