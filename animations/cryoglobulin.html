🔴 Cryoglobulinemic MPGN — Mechanism

Immune complex deposition → complement activation → MPGN

  • Trigger: Chronic antigenemia (HCV, IV drug use) → sustained immune stimulation
  • Cryoglobulins: IgM rheumatoid factor binds IgG → immune complexes that precipitate in cold
  • Deposition: Complexes deposit in glomerular capillaries → subendothelial deposits
  • Complement: Classical pathway activation → low C4 with normal C3
  • IF: Granular IgG, IgM, and C3 along capillary walls

🔴 Cryoglobulinemic MPGN — Clinical

Palpable purpura + MPGN + low C4 + RF positive

  • Skin: Palpable purpura (leukocytoclastic vasculitis) on lower extremities
  • Kidney: MPGN pattern — nephritic/nephrotic, active sediment (RBC casts, dysmorphic RBCs)
  • Serology: RF positive, low C4, normal C3, cryoglobulins positive
  • Associations: HCV (~80%), autoimmune disease, lymphoproliferative disorders
  • Treatment: Treat underlying cause (antivirals for HCV) + immunosuppression
🔴

Board Pearl — Low C4 + Normal C3 + RF Positive = Cryoglobulinemic MPGN

The combination of low C4 with normal C3 and a positive rheumatoid factor in a patient with palpable purpura and active urinary sediment is classic for mixed cryoglobulinemia. Biopsy shows MPGN with immunoglobulin deposits (IgG + IgM + C3). This is distinct from C3 glomerulopathy, which has dominant C3 and no immunoglobulins.
