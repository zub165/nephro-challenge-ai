🔵 Dent Disease — Pathophysiology

Defective proximal tubular endocytosis

  • Gene: CLCN5 (Dent-1, ~60%) or OCRL (Dent-2, ~15-20%) — X-linked recessive
  • Defect: Impaired megalin-cubilin receptor-mediated endocytosis in proximal tubule
  • Consequence: Failure to reabsorb filtered low molecular weight proteins and albumin
  • Urine: LMW proteinuria (RBP, α1-microglobulin, β2-microglobulin) + moderate albuminuria
  • Other features: Hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis, nephrolithiasis, glycosuria, aminoaciduria, proximal RTA

🔵 Why Moderate Albuminuria Is Expected

Albumin is reabsorbed via the same pathway

  • Shared pathway: Albumin and LMW proteins are both reabsorbed via megalin-cubilin endocytosis
  • Quantitative definition: Tubular proteinuria = elevated RBP + albumin < (10 × RBP) + 2[reference:0]
  • Prevalence: Albuminuria is common — 62% of Dent patients have moderate-to-severe albuminuria[reference:1]
  • Dipstick: May be negative because albumin concentration is below the detection threshold (dipstick primarily detects albumin, but tubular proteinuria is dominated by LMW proteins)
  • PCR: Elevated (1.5) due to the total protein load from LMW proteins and albumin
🔵

Board Pearl — Dent Disease = Tubular Proteinuria with Albuminuria

Dent disease causes defective proximal tubular endocytosis, leading to low molecular weight proteinuria AND moderate albuminuria. The urine dipstick may be negative because albumin is below the detection threshold, but the PCR is elevated due to LMW proteins. This is different from glomerular proteinuria, where the dipstick is positive. Moderate albuminuria is expected — it is part of the tubular proteinuria profile.
