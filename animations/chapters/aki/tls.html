🟣 G6PD Deficiency

The pentose phosphate pathway's protective enzyme

  • Function: G6PD generates NADPH → regenerates reduced glutathione → neutralizes reactive oxygen species
  • Inheritance: X-linked recessive — most common in Mediterranean, African, Middle Eastern, and Southeast Asian ancestry
  • Triggers: Sulfonamides, dapsone, primaquine, nitrofurantoin, fava beans, infection, rasburicase
  • Presentation: Acute intravascular hemolysis, hemoglobinuria, jaundice, anemia
  • Key clue in this case: Prior sulfonamide-induced anemia → strongly suggests undiagnosed G6PD deficiency

🔴 Rasburicase

Recombinant urate oxidase — powerful but dangerous in G6PD

  • Mechanism: Converts uric acid → allantoin (highly soluble, easily excreted)
  • Byproduct: Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) — a reactive oxygen species
  • Uses: Tumor lysis syndrome (especially in high-risk hematologic malignancies)
  • Contraindications: G6PD deficiency, known allergy, pregnancy
  • If G6PD-deficient: Use allopurinol (or febuxostat) + hydration + close monitoring
🚨

Board Pearl — Test G6PD BEFORE Rasburicase

Rasburicase generates H₂O₂ as a byproduct. In G6PD-deficient patients, RBCs cannot neutralize H₂O₂ → severe oxidative hemolysis and methemoglobinemia. The patient's prior sulfonamide-induced anemia is a major red flag. Screen G6PD before giving rasburicase.

✅ Quick Check — 5 Questions

1. Which test should be ordered before giving rasburicase?

G6PD assay. Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency because it generates H₂O₂, which triggers severe hemolysis.

2. Why is rasburicase dangerous in G6PD deficiency?

Correct. Rasburicase converts uric acid to allantoin and produces H₂O₂. G6PD-deficient RBCs lack the NADPH needed to neutralize it → oxidative hemolysis.

3. Which historical clue suggests this patient has G6PD deficiency?

Correct. Sulfonamides are oxidative drugs — classic triggers of hemolytic crisis in G6PD-deficient patients.

4. What is the appropriate alternative if the patient IS G6PD-deficient?

Allopurinol + hydration. Allopurinol inhibits xanthine oxidase without producing H₂O₂ — safe in G6PD deficiency. Avoid rasburicase and pegloticase.

5. Which lab pattern is classic for tumor lysis syndrome?

↑K⁺, ↑PO₄, ↓Ca, ↑uric acid. Classic TLS: hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia (Ca binds phosphate), and hyperuricemia.
