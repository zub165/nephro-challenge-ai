🔴 Cast Nephropathy — Mechanism

Tubular obstruction by light chain casts

  • Light chain: Kappa more common than lambda (this patient: κ/λ = 8.2)
  • Binding: Kappa LC + Tamm-Horsfall protein → insoluble casts in distal tubule
  • Location: Distal tubules (T-H protein is secreted here)
  • Consequence: Tubular obstruction → acute kidney injury
  • Urine: Hyaline casts; on biopsy: fractured casts with giant cell reaction

🔴 Cast Nephropathy — Clinical Pattern

AKI + light chain proteinuria + lytic lesions

  • Dipstick vs PCR mismatch: Trace protein on dipstick but high PCR — the protein is light chains, not albumin
  • Urine microscopy: Hyaline casts, tubular epithelial cells
  • Context: High tumor burden (lytic bone lesions, anemia)
  • Treatment: Aggressive hydration + treat the plasma cell clone
  • Note: Kappa clone argues against AL amyloidosis (which is usually lambda)

Board Pearl — Kappa + Light Chain Proteinuria + AKI = Cast Nephropathy

The trace dipstick + high PCR pattern is the clue — the protein is light chains, not albumin. Combined with a kappa-predominant clone and AKI, the biopsy will show fractured tubular casts with giant cell reaction. Kappa clone argues against AL amyloidosis (which is usually lambda).
