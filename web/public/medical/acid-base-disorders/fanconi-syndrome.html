Check Your Understanding

1. A 67-year-old man with anemia, back pain, and renal insufficiency has a normal anion gap metabolic acidosis (HCO₃⁻ 18, Cl⁻ 110), urine pH 5.0, and 1+ glycosuria with a normal serum glucose of 86 mg/dL. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. The combination of normal anion gap metabolic acidosis, acidic urine (pH < 5.5), glycosuria with normal serum glucose, and a dipstick-negative but high protein/Cr ratio (light chains) is classic for proximal RTA/Fanconi syndrome. In an older man with anemia and back pain, this points to multiple myeloma.

2. Why does the urine dipstick show negative protein while the urine protein/creatinine ratio is 2 g/g?

Correct: B. Standard urine dipsticks are designed to detect albumin and can miss light chains (Bence Jones proteins). In multiple myeloma, the urine contains a large amount of monoclonal light chains, so the protein/Cr ratio is high while the dipstick may be negative or only trace.

3. What is the anion gap in this patient (Na⁺ 138, Cl⁻ 110, HCO₃⁻ 18)?

Correct: B. Anion gap = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻) = 138 − (110 + 18) = 10, which is normal (reference range ~8–12). Therefore, this is a normal anion gap (hyperchloremic) metabolic acidosis, not a high-anion gap acidosis.

4. Which urine anion gap finding is most consistent with proximal RTA?

Correct: B. In proximal RTA, the distal tubule can still acidify the urine once the serum HCO₃⁻ falls below the proximal reabsorptive threshold (which is why the urine pH can be < 5.5). The urine anion gap may be negative or variable, unlike distal RTA which typically shows a strongly positive UAG due to impaired ammonium excretion.

5. Which of the following is NOT part of the Fanconi syndrome?

Correct: D. Fanconi syndrome is characterized by proximal tubular dysfunction with glycosuria, phosphaturia, aminoaciduria, proximal RTA, and hypokalemia (not hyperkalemia). Hyperkalemia is more typical of Type 4 RTA.

6. A patient with known multiple myeloma presents with bone pain, anemia, and renal failure. Which renal lesion is most likely?

Correct: C. Multiple myeloma can cause several renal lesions, including light chain proximal tubulopathy (Fanconi syndrome), cast nephropathy, AL amyloidosis, and light chain deposition disease. The presence of glycosuria with normal serum glucose and normal anion gap metabolic acidosis in this case points to proximal tubular dysfunction.
