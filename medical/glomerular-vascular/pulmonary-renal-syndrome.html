Check Your Understanding

1. A 41-year-old woman presents with AKI, hematuria with RBC casts, and bilateral interstitial lung edema. Biopsy shows crescentic GN and linear IgG on IF. What is the diagnosis?

Correct: C. The combination of crescentic GN on LM, linear IgG along the GBM on IF, and a pulmonary-renal syndrome is diagnostic of Anti-GBM disease. When associated with pulmonary hemorrhage, it is called Goodpasture's syndrome.

2. What is the pathognomonic IF finding in anti-GBM disease?

Correct: B. The classic IF pattern in anti-GBM disease is linear IgG deposition along the GBM. This reflects autoantibodies directed against the non-collagenous domain of the α3 chain of type IV collagen.

3. Which IF pattern is characteristic of ANCA-associated vasculitis?

Correct: C. ANCA-associated vasculitis causes a pauci-immune crescentic GN, meaning IF shows little to no immunoglobulin or complement deposition. Serology for ANCA (anti-PR3 or anti-MPO) is positive.

4. What is the initial treatment of choice for anti-GBM disease with pulmonary hemorrhage?

Correct: C. Anti-GBM disease is treated with plasmapheresis (to remove autoantibodies) combined with immunosuppression (cyclophosphamide + high-dose corticosteroids). Rituximab is a second-line option. Prompt treatment is critical to preserve kidney function.

5. Which IF pattern is seen in class IV lupus nephritis?

Correct: B. Lupus nephritis typically shows a granular "full house" pattern on IF, with deposition of IgG, IgA, IgM, C3, and C1q along the capillary loops and mesangium. This reflects the polyclonal immune complex nature of the disease.

6. A patient presents with palpable purpura, neuropathy, and MPGN on biopsy with tram-tracking. What is the most likely underlying cause?

Correct: C. Cryoglobulinemic GN typically presents as an MPGN pattern on LM with tram-tracking / double contours, and is strongly associated with Hepatitis C infection. Palpable purpura and neuropathy are classic extrarenal features.
