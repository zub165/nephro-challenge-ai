Check Your Understanding

1. Which test is MOST useful to confirm the diagnosis in this patient?

Correct: C. The patient has SLE features but presents with a bland urinary sediment and TMA on biopsy. This points away from Lupus Nephritis (which causes active sediment and immune complex deposition) and toward secondary Antiphospholipid Syndrome (APS) causing renal TMA. Testing for antiphospholipid antibodies (Lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2GPI) is essential.

2. Why is Anti-dsDNA (Choice B) less useful here?

Correct: It supports SLE but does not explain the TMA. While Anti-dsDNA is a marker of SLE, it does not directly diagnose APS. The patient's kidney biopsy shows TMA, not the typical immune complex-mediated lupus nephritis. Therefore, you must specifically test for APS.

3. What is the key urinary finding that points AWAY from classic Lupus Nephritis?

Correct: Bland urinary sediment. Classic Lupus Nephritis typically presents with active urinary sediment (dysmorphic RBCs, RBC casts, leukocytes). This patient has isomorphic RBCs and only trace blood, which is characteristic of a vascular/TMA process rather than active glomerulonephritis.
