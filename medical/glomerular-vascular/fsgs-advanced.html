Key Features: Differential Diagnosis & Treatment

Condition Light Microscopy (LM) Electron Microscopy (EM) Steroid Response Treatment After Steroid Failure
Steroid-Resistant Primary FSGS Correct Segmental sclerosis in >1 glomerulus; hyalinosis; adhesion to Bowman's capsule Foot process effacement; no deposits No response after 16 weeks of prednisone Calcineurin inhibitor (cyclosporine or tacrolimus) ± low-dose prednisone
Steroid-Sensitive Primary FSGS Same as above Foot process effacement; no deposits Remission within 16 weeks Taper steroids; consider CNI if relapsing
Minimal Change Disease Distractor Normal glomeruli Diffuse (100%) foot process effacement Highly steroid-sensitive (>90%) Steroid taper; consider CNI, MMF, or rituximab if frequent relapses
Membranous Nephropathy Distractor Thickened GBM; spikes on silver stain Subepithelial deposits; effacement Variable (~30% spontaneous remission) RAAS blocker; if high risk, rituximab or cyclophosphamide + steroids
Secondary FSGS Distractor Segmental sclerosis; larger glomeruli; adaptive changes Foot process effacement (less diffuse) Steroid-resistant Treat underlying cause; RAAS blocker; avoid immunosuppression

Check Your Understanding

1. A patient with primary FSGS completes 16 weeks of high-dose prednisone with no reduction in proteinuria. What is the BEST next step?

Correct: C. After 16 weeks without response, the patient has steroid-resistant FSGS. KDIGO guidelines recommend switching to a calcineurin inhibitor, with or without low-dose prednisone.

2. What is the maximum recommended duration of high-dose glucocorticoid therapy for primary FSGS?

Correct: C. KDIGO recommends a maximum of 16 weeks of high-dose glucocorticoid therapy. If there is no response by then, alternative therapy should be initiated.

3. Why is cyclophosphamide NOT the preferred next step after steroid failure in primary FSGS?

Correct: A. Cyclophosphamide carries significant toxicity (infertility, hemorrhagic cystitis, malignancy) with limited evidence in steroid-resistant primary FSGS, making CNIs the preferred option.

4. Which nephrotic syndrome pattern shows completely normal light microscopy with diffuse foot process effacement on EM?

Correct: B. MCD is characterized by normal LM and diffuse (100%) foot process effacement on EM. It is highly steroid-sensitive.

5. In secondary FSGS (e.g., from obesity, reflux, or reduced nephron mass), what is the role of immunosuppression?

Correct: B. Secondary FSGS is adaptive. Immunosuppression is not effective and should be avoided; instead treat the underlying cause and use RAAS blockade.
