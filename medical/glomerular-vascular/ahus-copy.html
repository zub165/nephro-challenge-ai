🔴 Atypical HUS (aHUS)

Thrombotic microangiopathy from endothelial complement injury

  • Mechanism: Uncontrolled alternative pathway → MAC on endothelium
  • Histology: Thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) — vessel wall thickening, thrombi
  • Labs: Schistocytes, thrombocytopenia, ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin, AKI
  • Trigger: Complement factor mutations (CFH, CFI, MCP, C3, CFB), anti-CFH antibodies
  • ADAMTS13: Normal (distinguishes from TTP)
  • Treatment: Eculizumab (anti-C5)

🔵 C3 Glomerulopathy (C3GN)

C3-dominant deposition within the glomerulus

  • Mechanism: C3 convertase dysregulation → C3b deposition in glomeruli
  • Histology: Often MPGN pattern (some cases); C3-dominant IF staining
  • Labs: Low C3, proteinuria, hematuria, variable CKD
  • Trigger: C3 nephritic factor (C3Nef), complement gene mutations, factor H deficiency
  • ADAMTS13: Normal (no TMA)
  • Treatment: Supportive, RAAS blockade; MMF or eculizumab in select cases

🟣 The Unifying Concept

Despite very different clinical and histological presentations — TMA in aHUS vs. glomerular C3 deposition in C3GN — both syndromes share a single underlying mechanism: dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway. This is why the correct answer to the exam question is C.

✅ Quick Check — 5 Questions

1. Which pathway is shared by aHUS and C3GN?

Alternative. Both syndromes are driven by dysregulation of the alternative complement pathway.

2. What histological pattern is characteristic of aHUS?

TMA. aHUS shows vessel wall thickening and thrombi — not MPGN. C3GN may show MPGN in some cases.

3. Which autoantibody stabilizes C3 convertase in C3GN?

C3Nef. C3Nef stabilizes C3 convertase → uncontrolled alternative pathway activity → C3GN.

4. What is ADAMTS13 activity in aHUS vs. TTP?

Normal in aHUS; low in TTP. Low ADAMTS13 is the defining feature of TTP — NOT aHUS or C3GN.

5. Which of the following is TRUE about aHUS and C3GN?

Correct. Both aHUS and C3GN — despite different clinical pictures — are driven by alternative pathway dysregulation.
