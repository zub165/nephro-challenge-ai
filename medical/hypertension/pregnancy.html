🟣 Preeclampsia — Why Highest Risk

Shared pathophysiology amplifies risk

  • Meta-analysis: Diabetic nephropathy is associated with a 10-fold increased risk of preeclampsia (OR 10.76)
  • Absolute incidence: Clinical series report preeclampsia rates exceeding 50% in pregnant women with diabetic nephropathy
  • Shared mechanism: Both preeclampsia and diabetic nephropathy involve endothelial dysfunction, impaired placentation, and angiogenic imbalance (sFlt-1/PlGF)
  • Amplifying factors: Pre-existing hypertension (148/92), proteinuria (2.8 g), and reduced eGFR (38) all compound the risk
  • Consequence: Preeclampsia then drives many of the other complications — preterm delivery, fetal growth restriction, and renal decline

📊 Comparative Risks

Relative risk for each complication

  • Preeclampsia: OR 10.76 — highest
  • Preterm delivery <34 wks: OR 6.90 (incidence ~70% <37 wks)
  • Progression to ESKD: >40% if Cr ≥1.4 mg/dL — very high absolute risk, but relative risk is lower than preeclampsia
  • Congenital malformations: OR 2.71, ~8% incidence — lowest relative risk of the four; tied to periconceptional HbA1c
  • Take-home: Preeclampsia is both the highest relative risk and the driver of downstream complications
🟣

Board Pearl — Diabetic Nephropathy + Pregnancy = Preeclampsia is the #1 Risk

Preeclampsia is the highest risk in pregnant women with diabetic nephropathy — 10-fold increased odds (OR 10.76) with an absolute incidence >50%. Pre-existing hypertension and proteinuria compound the risk. While ESKD progression (>40%) and preterm delivery (OR ~6.9) are also very high, preeclampsia carries the greatest relative risk and drives the others.
