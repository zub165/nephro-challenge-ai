Key Features: Differential Diagnosis of Renovascular Hypertension

Disease Typical Patient Angiographic Pattern Systemic Features Key Clue
Fibromuscular Dysplasia Correct Young–middle-aged women (20–50 y) "String of beads" — multiple alternating stenoses and post-stenotic dilatations in the mid-to-distal renal artery Usually isolated renovascular hypertension; may have headache, malignant HTN Young woman + severe HTN + classic beaded renal artery = FMD
Polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN) Distractor Middle-aged men, often with hepatitis B Multiple microaneurysms (1–5 mm), often bilateral, in medium-sized arteries — not a "string of beads" Fever, weight loss, neuropathy, livedo reticularis, multi-organ involvement Multi-organ involvement + microaneurysms on angiography
Giant Cell Arteritis (GCA) Distractor Older adults >50 y Smooth tapered stenosis of large arteries, often aortic involvement; renal artery involvement is rare Headache, jaw claudication, vision loss, polymyalgia rheumatica Age >50 + cranial symptoms + elevated ESR/CRP
Scleroderma Kidney Distractor Patients with systemic sclerosis (skin thickening) "Pruned tree" — diffuse distal intra-renal arterial narrowing with cortical thinning Raynaud's, sclerodactyly, skin thickening, interstitial lung disease Scleroderma skin changes + scleroderma renal crisis (severe HTN, AKI, MAHA)
Page Kidney Distractor Any age, often after trauma or biopsy Perirenal hematoma compressing the kidney; renal artery is normal History of flank trauma, biopsy, or anticoagulation Mass effect on imaging + history of trauma

Check Your Understanding

1. A 23-year-old woman presents with severe hypertension, blurry vision, and weight loss. CTA shows a "string of beads" appearance in the right renal artery. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: B. The "string of beads" appearance on angiography is the classic finding of fibromuscular dysplasia, especially in young women with renovascular hypertension. The beading reflects alternating areas of stenosis (from fibroplasia) and post-stenotic dilatation.

2. Which finding on angiography is most characteristic of polyarteritis nodosa?

Correct: B. PAN is a necrotizing vasculitis of medium-sized arteries that produces multiple small microaneurysms (1–5 mm) on angiography. The pattern is not the classic "string of beads" of FMD, which are alternating stenoses and dilatations.

3. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management for FMD?

Correct: C. FMD is a non-inflammatory vascular disease, so immunosuppression (steroids, cyclophosphamide) is not effective. The treatment of choice for renovascular hypertension due to FMD is percutaneous transluminal renal angioplasty (PTRA) without stenting, which cures or improves hypertension in most patients.

4. A 68-year-old man with new headache, jaw claudication, and vision changes is found to have a smooth stenosis of the renal artery on CTA. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Correct: C. GCA is a large-vessel vasculitis that occurs in patients >50 years and classically presents with headache, jaw claudication, and vision loss. It causes smooth tapered stenoses in large arteries (including the aorta and its branches), in contrast to the beaded pattern of FMD or the microaneurysms of PAN.

5. Which pattern on renal angiography is characteristic of scleroderma renal crisis?

Correct: C. Scleroderma renal crisis is characterized by a "pruned tree" appearance on angiography — diffuse narrowing of the distal intra-renal arteries with cortical thinning due to intimal proliferation and vasospasm.

6. A young athlete develops severe hypertension after a football injury to his flank. Imaging shows a large perirenal fluid collection compressing the kidney. What is the diagnosis?

Correct: D. A Page kidney occurs when a perirenal hematoma (usually post-traumatic) compresses the renal parenchyma, causing renal ischemia and activation of the renin-angiotensin system → severe hypertension. The renal artery itself is normal on angiography.
